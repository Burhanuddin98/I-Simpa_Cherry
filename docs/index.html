Simulation

It simulates room acoustics: Give it a 3D model of a room, assign materials to its surfaces and place sources and receivers. Cherry runs I-Simpa's solvers and shows how the sound fills the space: the level on cutting planes, band by band, and the acoustic parameters at every receiver.

You can watch it happen: Play the particles back step by step and see sound leave the source, strike the walls and spread through the room.

Workflow

Five steps: Geometry, Materials, Sources & receivers, Simulate, Results. The tabs along the top are the whole workflow, and each one says whether it is done. Three tutorial rooms are built in.

Checked: The model is checked for a closed volume and self-intersections before a run, and every result is checked before it is shown. A value that can't be trusted is refused rather than printed.

GPU Solver

SPPS on the GPU: I-Simpa's particle solver also runs on NVIDIA graphics cards. Choose it in the Simulate step. Without an NVIDIA card, everything runs on the CPU as before.

4 sinstead of 109 s with I-Simpa's own solver, in our test hall

Measured on one hall: two sources, 300,000 particles each, 10 s at 1 ms steps, 1 kHz, on an NVIDIA RTX 5070. Speed varies with the room and the card.

Details

Cherry is built around I-Simpa's solvers, with a new results layer on top.

SPPS particle tracing

I-Simpa's sound-particle solver follows energy through the room, so the geometry matters: balconies, coupled spaces, absorbing panels. On the CPU, or on an NVIDIA GPU.

TCR classical theory

Sabine and Eyring estimates from the same model, for a quick first look before a full particle run.

Acoustic parameters

Computed to ISO 3382-1 and IEC 60268-16, and checked against exact solutions to within the just-noticeable difference.

Hover a parameter for its meaning.

Maps

Sound level, T30, EDT, C80 and D50 drawn over cutting planes through the room, one octave band at a time.

Refused, not guessed

When a number would fall outside its valid range, the interface says so and why.
EDT REFUSED params not evaluable · several sources

Variants

Keep a baseline and try changes as variants. The cutting planes can show the difference from the baseline directly.

Listen

Hear a dry clip played through the room's response at a receiver.

Import and export

Open PLY, OBJ and STL models or an existing I-Simpa project. Export results as CSV, JSON, PNG or WAV.

Background

I-Simpa Cherry is written by Burhan Sakar at Dockyard Acoustics.

Its solvers come from I-Simpa, open-source room-acoustics software developed at Université Gustave Eiffel. All credit for them belongs to the I-Simpa team. I-Simpa Cherry is an independent project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the university.

The app is free to use. The solvers it runs are separate programs under their own licences, I-Simpa's solvers and the GPU solver under the GPL v3 and TetGen under the AGPL v3, and their complete source is published with each release.
